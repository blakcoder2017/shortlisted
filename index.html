01 — What is actually happening

You did not get rejected. You were never assessed.

When an advert says only shortlisted candidates will be contacted, that is literal. You will never receive a rejection — not for the roles you narrowly missed, not for the ones you were never in contention for.

So people apply thirty, forty times, hear nothing, and slowly conclude the problem is them. Usually it is not.

A panel scoring sixty applications works against a matrix drawn straight from the advert. Every requirement is a row. The shortlist has to be defensible in writing, often to a donor auditor reviewing the file years later.

Which means we can only credit what we can verify.

02 — The six-second difference

Same person. Same job. One gets the call.

What most graduates write

“Responsible for data collection and report writing on the project.”

0 Nothing here can be checked. It is not marked down — it is blank. The panel has no row to tick.
What gets you shortlisted

“Administered 212 household surveys across 9 communities in 6 weeks, 94% completion; resolved 41 duplicate records during cleaning.”

✓ Verifiable, specific, and assessable line by line. This is a candidate the panel can defend shortlisting.

You almost certainly have numbers like these. You have simply never counted them.

06 — Why me

Written by someone who reads the applications.

I work in monitoring and evaluation on EU-funded programmes in Northern Ghana. I write the Terms of Reference that become job adverts, I run procurement evaluations, and I sit on the panels that cut sixty applications down to six.

I kept watching capable people lose posts they could have done, for reasons that had nothing to do with ability — a CV with no numbers in it, a form field left blank, a file named wrongly, a desirable criterion they met and never mentioned.

Nobody was telling them. So I wrote it down.
